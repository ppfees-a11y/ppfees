A practical seller's guide to payment-processing costs

A payment fee is only one part of profitability. This guide explains how to price an order with fees in mind without mistaking gross sales for profit.

1. Identify how you actually receive payments

A PayPal Checkout payment, direct Goods & Services transfer, card payment, and point-of-sale transaction can carry different rates. Select the channel on your transaction statement instead of assuming all PayPal payments share one rate.

2. Separate sales revenue from take-home receipts

If a customer pays $100 and processing costs $3.98, the money received is $96.02. But that is not necessarily your profit: product cost, shipping, returns, marketplace commissions, taxes, advertising, and labor may still need to be deducted.

3. Account for the fixed component

When a channel charges a fixed amount per payment, splitting one $100 order into ten $10 orders can create ten fixed fees instead of one. This affects the economics of low-priced digital downloads, tips, and inexpensive products.

4. Calculate a price from a desired net amount

Instead of simply adding 3.49% to a target, reverse the fee correctly: gross = (target + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − percentage rate). Adding the rate to the target understates the gross amount because the fee is charged on the gross payment.

5. Think about margin, not just fees

Suppose a product sells for $50, costs $20 to deliver, and incurs $3 in other order costs. At 3.49% + $0.49, the estimated processing fee is $2.24. Estimated profit is $50 − $20 − $3 − $2.24 = $24.76. A discount or shipping change can alter that margin quickly.

6. Use volume to evaluate options

Monthly payment volume changes the importance of a rate difference. Compare methods using the same transaction size and count. Do not assume a lower advertised rate is available for your business or channel; eligibility and agreements matter.

7. Revisit rates when your workflow changes

If you move from payment links to card-present transactions, begin sending invoices, or start selling internationally, recalculate using the correct category. Also recheck the official merchant schedule when fees change.

8. What our calculator does not estimate

Our tool does not forecast fraud, chargebacks, refund policy impacts, tax treatment, conversion-rate changes, currency-exchange spread, merchant reserves, or future rate changes. Use it as a transparent planning aid, not an accounting statement.
