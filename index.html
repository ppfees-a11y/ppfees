Understanding PayPal Fees

PayPal fees are not a single universal percentage. For U.S. merchants, the cost can depend on how a customer pays and which PayPal product processes the transaction. A typical fee combines a percentage of the transaction with a fixed amount. Because of that fixed amount, the effective percentage cost is usually higher on small transactions than on large ones.

PayPal Checkout and Guest Checkout

For a common U.S. domestic commercial transaction using PayPal Checkout or PayPal Guest Checkout, PayPal's published merchant fee schedule lists 3.49% plus a fixed fee. For transactions received in U.S. dollars, the published fixed commercial-transaction fee is $0.49. PPFees includes that combination as a selectable estimate.

Credit and Debit Card Payments

PayPal's published U.S. merchant schedule distinguishes standard card payments from PayPal-branded checkout. Standard credit and debit card payments can therefore produce a different fee from a PayPal Checkout transaction even when the sale amount is identical.

International Commercial Transactions

International commercial transactions can carry an additional percentage-based fee on top of the applicable domestic rate. PPFees lets you add the published 1.50% international commercial surcharge to the selected base rate. Currency conversion and other charges may still apply and are not included here.

Why Reverse Fee Calculation Is Useful

Suppose you need to net a particular amount after processing costs. Simply adding the expected fee to your target is slightly inaccurate because the fee itself is calculated from the larger amount charged. The reverse calculator solves for the gross amount before the percentage and fixed fee are deducted.

Small Transactions and Fixed Fees

A fixed fee matters more on a small sale. For example, a $0.49 fixed component represents nearly one-half of one percent of a $100 transaction, but almost five percent of a $10 transaction before the percentage-based part is even considered. Sellers of low-priced items should pay particular attention to the effective fee rate.

How the Calculation Works

For a percentage-plus-fixed-fee transaction, the estimated fee equals the transaction amount multiplied by the percentage rate, plus the fixed fee. The estimated net amount is the transaction amount minus that fee. PPFees performs the arithmetic in your browser when you press the calculator button.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PPFees operated by PayPal?

No. PPFees is an independent educational calculator and is not PayPal, not endorsed by PayPal, and not a payment processor.

Are all PayPal fees 3.49% + $0.49?

No. PayPal publishes different rates for different payment products and transaction circumstances. That is why this calculator provides several choices instead of applying one rate to every transaction.

Does this calculator include currency conversion?

No. Currency conversion can involve separate pricing and depends on the currencies and transaction circumstances. This calculator focuses on the transaction-rate inputs shown in the tool.

Does the calculator send my numbers anywhere?

The fee calculations are performed in your browser. PPFees does not require an account or ask you to submit the transaction amounts to us.

Can I use a custom rate?

Yes. If your PayPal account or payment product uses a different percentage or fixed fee, choose Custom rate and enter those values.

Should I rely on PPFees for accounting or tax decisions?

No. PPFees is a convenience tool for estimates. For bookkeeping, tax reporting, contracts, or business decisions, use your actual PayPal transaction records and appropriate professional guidance.

Where can I verify current PayPal pricing?

Check PayPal's official U.S. merchant fee pages and the pricing shown for your own account before relying on an estimate.
